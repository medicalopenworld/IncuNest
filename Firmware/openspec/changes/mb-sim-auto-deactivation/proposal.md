## Why

Board affected: **motherBoard** only. `Display_HMI` and `shared/` are untouched, and no area with a documented bug in `Firmware/docs/known_issues.md` is involved (this change touches neither alarms, nor the HMI UART link, nor the boot/flash path).

Every deployed unit carries an Onomondo SIM that is activated on the factory floor by the `sim_act` factory test (`FTEST_MB_SIM_ACT`, `motherBoard/src/modules/factory_test/ftest_sim_activation.cpp`) and then keeps costing a monthly per-SIM fee for the rest of the unit's life. Once a unit is installed in a hospital with its own WiFi, that fee buys almost nothing: `GPRS_Handler()` already stops publishing telemetry over cellular as soon as `WIFIIsConnected()` is true (`motherBoard/src/tasks/GPRS.cpp:1314`), so the modem's only remaining job is keeping the GSM position and clock fresh. The fleet is paying a subscription per unit for a transport it no longer uses.

Nobody can currently decide that from the outside, because ThingsBoard has no idea **which** WiFi network a unit is on or **how long** it has been there. The SSID lives only in NVS (`NS_WIFI`/`KEY_SSID`, read at `motherBoard/src/tasks/Wifi_OTA.cpp:438-447`) and is never published. Uptime and hours of use are published, but they are the wrong signal: `in3.control_active_time` grows just the same during assembly, bench validation and the HMI training courses, so a unit can accumulate dozens of active hours before it ever leaves for the hospital. That is exactly the failure this change has to avoid — units are frequently assembled and commissioned at one site and only later moved to the hospital, and a SIM taken down at the assembly site leaves the unit with no connectivity at all for the trip and no way to bring itself back.

## What Changes

- **New WiFi dwell tracking on the motherBoard.** The board records which SSID it is associated with, when that SSID was first seen, and on how many distinct UTC days it has been seen since, persisted in NVS so it survives reboots and power cuts. Changing network resets the counter.
- **New ThingsBoard client attributes** published over the WiFi transport: the connected SSID, whether it is the compiled-in default (`WIFI_SSID`), the epoch of first association and the count of distinct days. These are the evidence the server needs and the only new firmware output this change exists to produce.
- **New coarse location from the public IP**, folded into the ip-api.com request that `ensureWifiTimeZoneSynced()` already makes once a day for the timezone (`motherBoard/src/tasks/Wifi_OTA.cpp:1284`). No new HTTP request and no new dependency: the `fields=` list grows. This exists because taking the SIM down removes the *only* position source the fleet has — `TX_FEATURE_TRIANGULATION_WIFI` is 0 (`motherBoard/include/config/transport_policy.h`) — and losing sight of where every unit is would be a worse outcome than the fee it saves.
- **Location becomes a two-source channel.** The existing `tri_latitud`/`tri_longitud`/`tri_accuracy` keys keep their meaning and stay preferred when the modem has a fix; the IP-derived position fills in only when it does not, and a new `loc_source` key says which one produced the published value so no dashboard has to guess.
- **The decision and the Onomondo call stay OFF the device.** The firmware never evaluates the deactivation policy and never talks to `api.onomondo.com` outside the factory test. `ONOMONDO_API_KEY` is a **fleet-wide** credential and `flasher_tool/data/firmware/*/firmware.bin` is versioned in a public repository, so shipping it in the field build would publish control of every SIM the organization owns. The server-side policy and its safety net are specified as a contract this change must satisfy, not as code it delivers — see Impact.
- Not breaking. No existing telemetry key changes meaning, no NVS key is repurposed, and a unit that never reaches a hospital WiFi behaves exactly as it does today.

## Capabilities

### New Capabilities
- `wifi-dwell-reporting`: what the motherBoard observes about its WiFi association and publishes to ThingsBoard — SSID identity and default-vs-provisioned classification, first-association epoch, the distinct-day counter and its reset rule, the clock-validity precondition for counting a day, NVS persistence and its wear budget, publication cadence and the fact that this path exists only on the WiFi transport. Also carries the **attribute contract** the server-side policy consumes, including the ≥14-day dwell threshold and the mandatory re-activation safety net, so the two halves cannot drift apart silently.
- `wifi-ip-geolocation`: the coarse position derived from the public IP on the existing daily ip-api.com lookup — the extended request, parsing and validation of an untrusted plaintext HTTP response, the precedence rule against the GSM fix, the accuracy value published for an IP-level position, the `loc_source` key, and what the fleet can and cannot conclude from a city-level fix.

### Modified Capabilities
(none — the existing specs in `openspec/specs/` are `factory-test-protocol`, `hmi-factory-test`, `hmi-help-center`, `hmi-link-loss-audio`, `hmi-maintenance-reminder` and `mb-factory-test`. None of their requirements change: the `sim_act` factory test keeps its behaviour, its states and its place in the battery untouched, and this change adds no field-side SIM control to it.)

## Impact

- **Affected code — motherBoard**: a new module for the dwell state (pure logic plus a thin NVS layer, so the decision arithmetic is host-testable); `src/tasks/Wifi_OTA.cpp` (the `ARDUINO_EVENT_WIFI_STA_GOT_IP` handler at `:128-137` gains the dwell update, `ensureWifiTimeZoneSynced()` at `:1240-1320` gains the location fields, and the attribute publish joins the existing `tb_wifi.sendAttributeJson()` path at `:1129`); `include/config/preferences_keys.h` (new `NS_WIFI` keys); `include/config/telemetry_keys.h` (the `loc_source` key and the attribute names).
- **Field budget**: `THINGSBOARD_FIELDS_AMOUNT` is 112 with ~13 slots of margin on the GPRS worst case (`transport_policy.h`); the new *attributes* travel in their own `sendAttributeJson()` payload and do not consume that budget, and the single new telemetry key is published on the WiFi path, which sits at ~81. `python tools/check_transport_matrix.py` must still pass.
- **Affected docs**: `Firmware/docs/transport_matrix.md` and `Firmware/docs/thingsboard_dashboards.md` (the new attributes, the two-source location channel, and the operational runbook for the server-side policy).
- **Server-side, outside this OpenSpec root** (`motherBoard`/`Display_HMI`/`shared` only) but a hard dependency of the feature actually working: a ThingsBoard rule chain or scheduled job that (1) deactivates the SIM with `PATCH /sims/{iccid}` `{"activated":false}` once a unit reports ≥14 distinct days on the same non-default SSID, (2) skips any SSID on an operator-maintained list of the organization's own networks, and (3) **re-activates the SIM automatically if the unit then goes silent for more than 72 h**. Point (3) is not optional: a unit that has lost its own SIM cannot restore it, so the undo has to live where the credential does. The API details are already established — `authorization: <key>` raw with no `Bearer`, and the ICCID works directly as `{id}` — see the header comment in `ftest_sim_activation.cpp`.
- **Residual risk accepted**: dwell time on one SSID is the sole trigger, so a commissioning or training stay that lasts longer than 14 days on a single network would take the SIM down early. The 72-hour re-activation net is what bounds the damage, which is why it is specified as a requirement of the attribute contract rather than left to the server integrator.
- **Testing**: the dwell state machine is pure logic — distinct-day counting across UTC midnight, the SSID-change reset, refusing to count while the clock is unset, and monotonicity across a reboot — and belongs in motherBoard's `[env:native]` with Unity tests, which requires extending `build_src_filter` in `motherBoard/platformio.ini` in the same commit per `.claude/rules/testing.md`. Parsing of the ip-api response is equally pure and equally testable, including truncated and hostile bodies. Everything with a radio in the loop is **manual verification only** and must be documented as such: a real association to a provisioned SSID, the attributes arriving in ThingsBoard, the location falling back when the SIM is down, and the end-to-end deactivation against a real Onomondo SIM.
- **Out of scope (Non-goals)**:
  - Any field-side call to the Onomondo API, and any presence of `ONOMONDO_API_KEY` in a non-factory build.
  - Re-activating the SIM from the device. It is impossible by construction — the device has no transport left to ask with.
  - Using position, hours of use or WiFi reliability as deactivation criteria. All three were considered and deliberately left out of the trigger; position is still published, and hours of use already are, so the server can add them later without a firmware change.
  - Reporting the WiFi password, the BSSID or a scan of neighbouring networks. Only the associated SSID is published.
  - Any change to the `sim_act` factory test or to how SIMs are activated on the factory floor.
